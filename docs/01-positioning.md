# 01 — Positioning

Source: `1is4me_Three_Doors_Blueprint_V3.pdf`, pages 01–03, 07–08.

## One-line identity

**1is4me — AI Growth Partner.**
*Learn It. Plan It. Build It.*

> Every business problem fits one of three doors — Education, Consulting,
> Implementation. One long-term partner behind all three.

## The core thesis

The differentiator is **not** the service list. Marketing, design, video and automation
are commodities; every AI agency sells them. The differentiator is the **diagnostic
frame** that comes before the service list.

Most competitors open with *what we do*. 1is4me opens with *where you're stuck*. The
buyer self-identifies before they ever see a deliverable or a price. That inversion is
the whole strategic asset — it should never be diluted by leading with capabilities.

### The three gaps

Owners aren't failing from lack of effort. They're stuck at one of three points, and
each needs a different kind of help.

| # | Gap | The owner's own words | What's actually happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Knowledge gap** | "I don't know what AI can do." | Paying people for work software could handle — because nobody ever showed them what's possible. |
| 02 | **Direction gap** | "I don't know what to fix first." | Money burns on random tactics because there's no diagnosis and no sequenced plan. |
| 03 | **Execution gap** | "I know what to do — but can't build it." | The plan exists. Nobody on the team can build or run the systems, so nothing ships. |

Each gap maps to exactly one door. The mapping is 1:1 and must stay that way — it's what
makes the frame legible in three seconds.

| Gap | Door | Closes it by |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | Education | Training owners and teams to understand and operate AI inside the business |
| Direction | Consulting | Diagnosis and strategy — find what's broken, decide what to fix first, build the plan |
| Execution | Implementation | Building it and running it — websites, video, marketing, automation, done for you |

## The four outcomes

The doors are *how we work*. These are *what the client buys*. Whichever door they enter,
they're buying the same four results:

| # | Outcome | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **More Clients** | Qualified buyers in the pipeline — not random traffic. |
| 02 | **Higher Profit** | Offers and funnels that convert attention into orders. |
| 03 | **Lower Costs** | AI doing the work you'd otherwise hire for. |
| 04 | **More Efficiency** | The owner out of the weeds — systems handle the repetition. |

These four are the only promises made anywhere on the site. Any new claim must reduce to
one of them or it doesn't ship.

## Who we serve

**Stated in V3:** Food & Drinks · Cafes & Restaurants · Fashion & Retail · Tech & SaaS ·
Real Estate · Grocery & Retail Chains.

> ⚠️ **Flagged.** Six unrelated verticals with zero case studies reads as *we've served
> nobody, so we'll say everybody*. Recommendation: cut the public list to the two or
> three verticals where real work exists, and expand only as proof accumulates. Tracked
> in [06-open-questions.md](06-open-questions.md#q3--vertical-list-is-unsupported).

**Buyer profile (inferred, needs confirmation):** owner-operator or founder of an SMB,
personally involved in day-to-day operations, spending on marketing without a diagnosis,
no in-house technical capability. The recurring phrase *"the owner out of the weeds"*
tells us the buyer is the person currently in the weeds — not a CMO, not a procurement
committee.

## Delivery model

Remote. Fast. No wasted calls.

Everything runs online — video calls, shared docs, async updates. **No geography
requirement, no office visits.** This is a positioning asset, not a limitation: it means
the addressable market isn't bounded by city, and it should be stated plainly rather
than hidden.

### The four-step engagement

| # | Step | Substance |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Discovery Call** | 15–30 minutes. Goals, current state, and whether it's a fit. No pitch deck. |
| 02 | **The Right Door** | We tell you honestly which of the three you actually need first. |
| 03 | **First Deliverable** | Something tangible in week one — a working piece, not a slide. |
| 04 | **Weekly Check-In** | Short call or async update. Momentum and visibility, every week. |

Two of these are load-bearing promises with real operational cost: *"we tell you
honestly which door you need"* (means turning away Implementation revenue when
Consulting is the right answer) and *"something tangible in week one"* (means week-one
delivery capacity must exist before the site goes live). Don't publish either unless
they will be honoured.

## Voice

Derived from the V3 copy itself, which is consistent enough to reverse-engineer:

- **Short declaratives.** "We build it and run it for you." "No pitch deck."
- **The buyer's words, not ours.** Gaps are quoted in first person, not described in
  jargon. Never write "digital transformation readiness assessment" where "I don't know
  what to fix first" exists.
- **Numbers over adjectives.** House rule, stated in the deck. "Five hires you'd
  otherwise need" beats "dramatically more efficient."
- **Name the alternative.** The strongest line in V3 works because it prices the status
  quo: *one AI-run team instead of the five hires you'd otherwise need.*
- **No hype vocabulary.** No "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "game-changing,"
  "unlock," "supercharge." The frame does the persuading; the adjectives would undercut it.
- **Honest about limits.** "We tell you honestly which door you need first" only reads as
  credible if the rest of the copy never oversells.

## What's missing from the positioning

Two structural gaps, both tracked in [06-open-questions.md](06-open-questions.md):

1. **No human.** No founder, no team, no origin story, no stated point of view. For a
   business whose central promise is a *long-term partnership*, the absence of a person
   is the single largest hole. Everything else in this deck is copyable by a competitor
   in a day; a founder with a track record is not.
2. **No proof.** Zero numbers, zero case studies, zero named clients. The blueprint is
   honest about this (it marks the slot rather than filling it), but honesty doesn't
   make the slot load-bearing any less. See
   [06 Q1](06-open-questions.md#q1--no-proof-exists-yet).
